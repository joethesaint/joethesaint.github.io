What my AI agents keep getting wrong

Four months of their work, read back across 29 projects. The system proposes rules from what it finds; I decide which ones stay.

Last week I shared the five rules I use to run a team of AI coding agents. This week I asked the agents a harder question: what do you keep getting wrong?

I had a system read back through four months of their work: every failed command, every blocked action, every correction I had to make.

What it found

  • 1,019mistakes, most of them small and repeatedthe same failure twice is the mistake
  • 70risky actions tried without asking, stopped only by a safety neta block is evidence the agent should have asked
  • 226needless pauses for my approval, caused by how a command was written, not because it was riskynone since 28 October

Three lessons

  1. 1The same failure twice is the mistake. A failing test while you build is just the work. The one worth fixing is the one that comes back.
  2. 2A safety-net block isn't friction to route around. It's evidence the agent should have asked first.
  3. 3A rule nobody measures is only a reminder. About 1 in 5 of my rules can be checked by code. Knowing which ones matters.

Who decides

The system proposes rules from what it finds. I decide which ones stay.
