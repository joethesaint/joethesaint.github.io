JOSEPH BAMISAYE · SOFTWARE & ENGINEERING

Biogas, rebuilt in the browser

I built this biogas project at FUTA. This viewer rebuilds the assembly as interactive software: inspect components, change views and explore it on your phone.

The engineering context

The school project explored biogas from cassava peel, pig excreta and poultry droppings. The reconstructed assembly connects a digestion tank, feeding funnel, three filter housings and tyre-tube storage.

The software I implemented

Procedural Three.js geometry, component picking, orthographic cameras and animated exploded views. React and Motion drive the touch controls, arc picker and liquid surfaces.

Try the evidence

Open the exploded assembly, tap a component, then switch between Front and Plan. These interactions show the modelling, camera control and state management directly.

The design decisions

Instanced fasteners reduce repeated draw calls. Mobile rendering budgets, on-demand scene drawing and reduced-motion support keep the interaction deliberate.

Engineering scope & comparison

B reconstructs the school assembly. A and C are concepts; D–F are reference systems; G is a Güstrow-inspired industrial schematic, not an exact site reconstruction. Geometry and fasteners are illustrative. This is not a dimensioned fabrication model. The process walkthrough explains the route and biological outputs; it does not simulate gas yield or treatment performance. Comparison scores describe design completeness, not measured gas production or validated performance.
THE SOFTWARE

Inside the viewer

JavaScript + Three.js, with React for loading and liquid controls. Procedural geometry, local dependencies, and a responsive interface.

Interaction

Raycasting selects components. An orthographic camera switches between an equal-axis isometric view and a plan view. OrbitControls supports touch rotation and pinch zoom.

Animation

Component groups use stored assembly positions and explosion offsets. One toggle animates between them. The process walkthrough highlights the active component and uses a GLSL shader for the gas-route particles; reduced-motion preferences make the sequence step-by-step.

Performance

Repeated fasteners use instanced meshes. Mobile pixel ratio and shadow resolution are capped, animation is limited to 30 FPS on mobile, hidden tabs skip scene drawing, and idle scenes avoid unnecessary drawing. These are rendering budgets, not a claim of measured frame rate.

Responsive layout

Controls float over the canvas. The movable menu opens inward; the arc mirrors its position. Component details adapt to small screens, and keyboard controls provide another way to explore.
